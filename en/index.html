ISO 14971

Risk management, linked to the graph

FMEA register (hazard → hazardous situation → harm), 5×5 matrix with Acceptable / ALARP / Unacceptable zones, initial risk against residual. A control can be linked to the specifications and tests that carry it.

  • Controls linked to the tests that verify them
  • 5×5 severity × probability matrix (+ detectability), hazard library
  • Risk management file (RMF) generated as .docx

On OD-300: R-1 “thermal energy” controlled and verified; R-4 “use error” still open — flagged as such.

CLSI EP

IVD method validation, statistics included

Import the bench CSV: the built-in statistical engine runs the CLSI protocols — EP05 precision (ANOVA), EP06 linearity, EP09 method comparison (Bland-Altman, Passing-Bablok), EP17 LoB/LoD/LoQ, EP25 stability, EP26 lot-to-lot — and returns a Pass, Fail or Inconclusive verdict. The study verifies a specification: the evidence lands in the matrix.

  • 6 CLSI protocols, statistics and charts computed internally
  • Automatic verdict — no spreadsheet on the side
  • Every study files its evidence into the traceability matrix

On NovaLyte: 6 specifications, 6 studies STU-1 to STU-6, each linked to its specification.

ISO 13485 §8.3 / §8.5

NC & CAPA born from the graph

From a failed test or study, a button raises the nonconformity linked to the graph; its disposition is signed afterwards. Escalation to CAPA is justified on risk, and the lifecycle follows ordered steps — no action plan without a root cause, and an effectiveness check step before closure.

  • Staged lifecycle: Open → Investigation → Plan → Implementation → Verification → Closure
  • Moves to verification only once every action is done or cancelled

On OD-300: the failed VER-3 test raised NC-1, escalated to CAPA-1 (root cause: unsuitable seal elastomer), closed EFFECTIVE.

Compliance of the software itself

Verifiable without us.

A compliance tool that asks to be taken at its word is worth nothing. Every mechanism below can be checked by a third party — often without even opening Filum.

Chained audit trail

Every entry carries the fingerprint of the previous one. Modifying a row directly in the database, or deleting a row that has others after it, creates a break, and verification says at which rank. This check has its limits: whoever writes to the database can recompute the chain, and the last rows of the trail can be deleted without a break. A comparison with an earlier archive can then show it, for what that archive covers. Chaining detects, it does not prevent — that is written as such on the screen.

Inspection archive verifiable without Filum

A compressed folder: the approved records byte for byte, their fingerprints, and a single anchor covering them all. Your auditor checks it with sha256sum, on any machine, ten years from now — without the software, without us, without a licence.

Third-party timestamping

The archive anchor is counter-signed by the RFC 3161 timestamping authority you designate. A chain recomputed after the fact will not give back the timestamped head. The token is verified outside Filum with openssl ts -verify: Filum does not check its signature itself.

Declared retention

Filum applies no purge at expiry. You declare your period, what grounds it and your procedure reference.

Nobody sets someone else's password

An administrator issues a single-use token; its holder chooses their own secret. Whoever types another person's password holds both components of their signature — exactly what 21 CFR 11.200(a)(3) sets out to prevent.

Attributable signatures, roles server-side

The password is asked again at every signature, and only the designated signer can apply theirs. Administrator, approver, editor, viewer: the guard is at the server, not in a greyed-out button.

What we never write: “21 CFR Part 11 compliant”.

The §11.100(c) certification binds the legal entity whose staff sign, by a letter with a handwritten signature to the FDA. No software vendor can carry it for you, and one that claims to is selling you a risk.

Scope

What Filum does. And what it does not.

A demonstration given to somebody looking for something else costs two hours on both sides. So we say it here. No line in the right-hand column comes with a date.

What is in place

  • TraceabilityGraph need → requirement → specification → test → evidence. Matrix and impact analysis generated, never stale.
  • Statement qualityDeterministic checks as you type, EARS templates, rewrite proposed — never written.
  • Document controlDraft → Review → Released. On entering review, the content is frozen into a file whose fingerprint is kept. A divergence during review blocks release; after release, the screen can drift from the signed record (statuses, test results), with no warning. Design reports, DHF, RMF, NCR, CAPA generated as .docx.
  • Electronic signatureTwo components at every signature, human-readable manifestation, fingerprint of the attested content on documents and audit trail reviews.
  • ISO 14971 riskFMEA, 5×5 matrix, ALARP. A control can be linked to the tests that carry it.
  • IVD method validationSix CLSI protocols computed internally — EP05, 06, 09, 17, 25, 26 — automatic verdict.
  • Nonconformities and CAPAStaged lifecycles, from detection to effectiveness check.
  • Technical fileThe package an inspector asks for: index over 50 regulatory items, statuses derived from the graph.
  • Software complianceChained AND REVIEWED audit trail, archive verifiable without Filum, RFC 3161 timestamping, declared retention, legal hold.
  • Migrating your dataYour Excel workbooks and Word documents with no prior conversion, links resolved by YOUR codes, mandatory dry run before any write.
  • OperationsSelf-hosted Docker, FR / EN interface, four roles.

What does not exist

  • This is not an eQMSNo training, no suppliers, no complaints, no internal audits, no management review.
  • No configuration managementNo baselines, no configuration items, no comparison between two frozen states.
  • IEC 62304 is a stubNo tooled software lifecycle, no anomaly tracking, no SOUP management.
  • No outbound channelFilum sends no email: no notifications, no due-date reminders, no approver chasing.
  • No integration with your information systemNo webhooks, no PLM / ERP / ALM connector, no ReqIF. Filum is not an ERP and does not stand in for one.
  • One installation, one organisationNot multi-tenant: isolation is per instance, one per entity.
  • You only sign what Filum producesNo signing of an uploaded PDF or a file coming from elsewhere.
  • Audit entries already written are not retranslatedThe trail is bilingual; what was recorded earlier stays in its own language.
  • Concurrent users are not qualifiedGrowth in volume is measured; concurrency is not.
  • No mobile app, no offline modeWeb application served by your own server, for a computer screen.

Filum will probably not suit you if you are looking for a complete quality system (training, suppliers, complaints, audits)  ·  your device is primarily software and you expect a tooled IEC 62304 chain  ·  you need a PLM, ERP or ALM integration  ·  you want a vendor-run multi-tenant SaaS  ·  you expect a supplier to declare itself “21 CFR Part 11 compliant”.

The scope note expands every line, states the three design trade-offs behind the absences, and says what remains your organisation's responsibility. No form, no email address to leave:

Questions to ask

What a quality manager would ask first.

Where is our data hosted?

With you: Filum installs locally or on your own server — your data lives in two Docker volumes (the database and the files, including approved documents), to be backed up together.

What about the text of ISO / IEC / ASTM standards?

Filum never hosts it: the library holds metadata only (code, title, usage description, official link). You read the text in the copies your company purchased — the standards bodies' copyright is respected by construction.

The software itself has to be validated on our side. Do you help?

Not today: the IQ protocol and the validation file are withdrawn (see the note above). And validation on your side remains yours: a vendor pack feeds it, it does not replace it.

In any case, these remain yours: the system criticality decision, performance qualification under your real conditions, and your revalidation policy.

How do we prove to an inspector that a record has not moved?

You produce an inspection archive: a compressed folder holding the approved records byte for byte, the list of their SHA-256 fingerprints, the audit trail, and a single anchor covering everything. The inspector verifies it with the standard tools of their operating system — without Filum, without a licence, without us. That anchor can be counter-signed by the RFC 3161 timestamping authority of your choice: a value recomputed after the fact will never give back the one that was timestamped.

The honest corollary: chaining detects a break, it does not prevent it. Anyone with direct access to the database file can write outside the application, and recompute the chain — third-party timestamping, and the access restriction that belongs to your infrastructure, are what close that door.

Can the AI write anything it likes into my file?

No. It receives only your graph's data, is instructed to cite its sources and to write “to be completed” when information is missing — and nothing enters a document without your named validation. No regulatory decision is automated.

We work in Word. Will we have to change?

No — quite the opposite. Filum generates .docx files structured like your templates (title block, approvals, revision history, Design Input/Output…). You keep delivering Word; you just stop re-typing it.
