Position on AI

A model is called at three places. None of them produces evidence.

Filum ships a pre-drafting assistant. It runs on your Anthropic key, it is disabled with one checkbox, and with no key configured the product works in full. Above all: nothing that constitutes evidence goes through it — traceability, signatures, the chained audit trail, the inspection archive and the 11 statement quality checks are deterministic and run offline. That is what makes them qualifiable, and 43 cases do it.

Model called today: claude-opus-5. That name is written into every retained draft, and it is never rewritten retroactively.

The three call sites, and nothing else

This answers a data protection officer's question: what leaves, when, and where does it go.

Pre-drafting assistant, on a document

What goes out
The graph nodes attached to THIS document: codes, statements, measured values, statuses. Nothing else — not the audit trail, not the user accounts, not the neighbouring documents.
What comes back
A draft synthesis or conclusion, stored apart from the document, marked “proposed”.
The lock
The draft retains the EXACT LIST of nodes it was given and the model's name. It enters the document only if a named person accepts it, and that acceptance is dated and logged. A rejected draft is retained: the trace of what was proposed is part of the record.

Requirement rewrite, as you type

What goes out
The statement you have just typed, and the list of form defects raised by the deterministic checks. Nothing from the rest of the file.
What comes back
A proposed rewrite, displayed next to the field.
The lock
Nothing is written. The proposal is displayed, you copy back what you keep — it is your keystrokes that enter the record. The model is furthermore forbidden to invent a value, a unit or a component absent from your statement: where your sentence is not measurable, it leaves a placeholder to fill rather than a plausible figure.

Connection test, in Settings

What goes out
A minimal message, with no data from your file. It only checks that the key answers.
What comes back
An acknowledgement, or the reason for refusal.
The lock
Not applicable: nothing is produced, nothing is retained.

Six locks, verifiable in the delivered product

Each names the place where you can see it for yourself. A guarantee you cannot verify is not a guarantee.

1

AI signs nothing

No text produced by a model enters a record without a named person having accepted it, and that acceptance carries their name, its date and its entry in the audit trail.

An electronic signature engages a person's accountability (21 CFR 11.50). Text slipped into an approved document without passing through somebody would make that signature untruthful.

↳ Document screen: the draft is displayed separately, with its “accept” and “reject” buttons.

2

The model invents nothing

The system instruction forbids inventing a value, a result or a reference, requires quoting each source's exact code in brackets, and requires writing “to be completed” where information is missing.

An invented figure has no source, and it is the source that holds a design file together. A gap filled by a guess is worse than a gap: it can no longer be seen.

↳ The instruction is in the delivered code, readable as is. We will show it to you on request.

3

The model returns no verdict

It is explicitly forbidden to conclude on the conformity or non-conformity of anything. It states what the data shows; the decision belongs to the signatory.

A regulatory verdict binds the organisation before an authority. It is not delegated to a tool, least of all a tool whose output varies from one call to the next.

↳ Same system instruction. A draft containing a verdict can be rejected like any other.

4

Your key, your contract

Filum ships no API key. It is YOUR Anthropic key that is used, stored in your local database, never in our code and never in our repository. With no key configured, no call goes out — and the product works in full.

A vendor key would route your design data through our account, under our terms, at our discretion. No design file should depend on a supplier's commercial relationship with a third party.

↳ Settings → AI assistant. The key's source is displayed there: your organisation, the server environment, or none.

5

One switch, and it really cuts

An administrator disables the assistant for the whole installation. No call goes out then, even if a key remains configured.

Some organisations forbid any external call from systems holding design data. They must be able to enforce it, not ask their users for it.

↳ Settings → AI assistant → “enable” checkbox.

6

What matters most never calls it

Traceability, signatures, content freezing, the chained audit trail, the inspection archive, the statement quality checks: none of them calls a model. They are deterministic, they run offline, and they are qualified.

You cannot qualify what does not return the same result twice. Everything carrying regulatory proof is therefore, by construction, outside the model's scope.

↳ The validation file: every case runs without an API key, and the whole suite runs offline.

What we do not guarantee

The part brochures leave out. An auditor asking for the contract would find the gap in one question — better we write it ourselves.

Use of your data by the model provider

Filum calls the API with YOUR key. The applicable terms — retention, use for training, processing location — are those of YOUR contract with Anthropic, not ours. We cannot commit on a third party's behalf, and a promise we do not hold would serve you nothing before an auditor. Read that contract, and if your policy requires it, leave the assistant disabled: the product works in full without it.

The quality of what the model proposes

A language model returns plausible text, not true text. The instructions reduce the risk of invention; they do not remove it. That is exactly why nothing enters a record without human reading, and why the draft retains the list of sources it received: so that checking is possible, and quick.

Reproducibility of a model output

Two identical calls do not necessarily return the same text. That is a property of the model, not a flaw in Filum — and it is why no qualified function depends on it. What is retained, by contrast, is perfectly reproducible: the accepted draft is frozen, and its digest enters the record like any other content.

A validation of the model itself

We do not validate Anthropic, and nobody can in the ISO 13485 sense. What we validate is the SOFTWARE AROUND IT: the locks above are within the qualification scope, the model is not. If an auditor asks you for the AI's validation, that is the honest answer — and it holds, because nothing that constitutes proof goes through the model.

The questions we get asked

Can I use Filum with no AI at all?

Yes, and that is the default state: with no key configured, no call goes out. Every regulatory function — traceability, signatures, audit trail, archive, statement checks — works identically.

Can the AI modify a requirement already written?

No. No path in the product lets a model write into the graph. The requirement rewrite is displayed next to the field; you are the one who copies it back.

How do I prove to an auditor that a text was not written by AI without anyone knowing?

Each retained draft carries its origin — the model's name — the list of sources it was given, the person who accepted or rejected it, and the date. Whatever did not go through that was typed by somebody.

Does the model name change retroactively?

No. A draft keeps the name of the model that WROTE it, even after an update. Recomputing that field on display would say what we use today instead of what wrote the text — and a record is not rewritten.
